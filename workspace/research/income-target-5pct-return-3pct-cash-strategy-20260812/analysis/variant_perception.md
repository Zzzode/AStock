# Variant Perception — An Open Universe Is Not an Open Allocation Mandate

## Widely inferred views

Two opposing shortcuts are common:

1. A fixed high-dividend ETF / public-REIT menu should mechanically deliver
   cash and therefore deserves a permanent allocation.
2. Allowing direct equities into the strategy means the portfolio should pivot
   to individual stocks immediately.

Both shortcuts confuse research eligibility with investment approval.

## AStock research view

The product menu should be open at research intake and closed only at the
decision and investment stages. A direct equity, ETF, public REIT, sovereign
instrument or cash product should compete on the same decision dimensions:
source quality, economic exposure, current valuation, tax- and cost-aware
settled cash, downside stress, common-risk overlap, liquidity/capacity and
suitability.

This supports flexibility without turning a dividend label, screen result or
market move into an allocation permission. The ten existing products remain a
useful first-round evidence queue, but none is privileged and none is approved.

## Strongest opposing argument

An open universe can become a vehicle for unconstrained narrative selection,
yield chasing, issuer concentration and scope drift. Direct high-dividend
stocks can add financial, cyclical, governance, suspension and liquidity risk
that overlaps the existing dividend ETF and REIT exposures. In particular,
opening the universe without a finite decision set would recreate the
unresearchable “unnamed category” problem.

## Falsification and monitoring

The architecture should be revised if either of these is demonstrated:

- a fixed category vector outperforms an open, evidence-gated cross-asset
  decision process after a reproducible point-in-time, cost/tax/capacity-aware
  comparison; or
- a proposed direct equity fails to improve the approved portfolio's marginal
  total-return/risk/liquidity profile despite a strong standalone narrative.

For every future candidate, monitor evidence freshness, financial/operating
delivery, valuation assumptions, cash/dividend coverage, governance events,
liquidity, concentration, common-risk mapping and the portfolio risk-budget
state. An expired packet, a failed assumption or an execution constraint
returns the candidate to q=0.

## Boundary

The framework can promise only an open nomination process, item-level
institutional diligence and transparent measurement of objective outcomes.
It cannot promise an annual 5% return, asset-value protection, unchanged
distributions or allocation approval.
