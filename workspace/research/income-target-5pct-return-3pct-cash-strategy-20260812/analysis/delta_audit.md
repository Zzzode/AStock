# Delta Audit

## Original scope correction

The prior strategic allocation did not support a defensible total-return
objective. It treated a low-risk product vector as if it could answer a
portfolio question without a frozen cross-asset universe, current valuation,
cost-aware return evidence or a compatible risk budget.

## Earlier repairs retained

The report now has an open T0/T1 research boundary, a finite dated T2 decision
universe, item-level T3 approval and a separately approved dynamic T4
portfolio. Direct equities, ETFs, public REITs, sovereign instruments and cash
tools can compete only after structure-appropriate research. The old fixed
vector is retired because its known planning-stress subtotal breached the
existing risk-action boundary.

TWRR/MWRR separation, external-flow unitization, tax/fee/settlement records,
capital-repayment classification and no-double-counting controls remain
required. They are accounting and governance controls, not evidence of an
approved allocation.

## Current mandate correction

The earlier revision incorrectly retained a second cash-receipt objective after
the requirement to remove it. This version restores a single conditional
objective: rolling-five-year, annualized, cost-aware TWRR of 5%. Cash
distributions are components of total return and a receipt ledger is still
required, but there is no cash-yield target, coverage screen, asset-class quota,
annual cash success/failure status or target-repair allocation.

The investor remains free to retain, withdraw or take no action with a receipt.
Any later market deployment is a new T1-to-T4 proposal; it is not automatic or
mandatory.

## Prevention rule

Any future strategic-allocation report must state: (1) the single performance
objective and its measurement convention; (2) whether cash distributions are
only total-return components or a separately authorized mandate; (3) whether
the framework is scale-neutral; (4) T0/T1/T2/T3/T4 approval architecture; (5)
TWRR/MWRR and external-flow treatment; (6) receipt, tax, settlement,
capital-repayment and cash-disposition accounting; (7) candidate assets and
concentration proof; (8) formal return-evidence and benchmark status; (9)
risk-budget compatibility; and (10) whether any stated figure is a conditional
objective rather than a guarantee.
