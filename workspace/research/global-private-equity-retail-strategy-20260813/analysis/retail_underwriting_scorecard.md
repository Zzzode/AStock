# Retail underwriting scorecard — five votes, all required

This is a generic research gate, not a recommendation to buy a security. A candidate enters a **researchable** list only when each vote passes; a named allocation still needs the additional risk, account and liquidity approval in the return-model packet.

## Vote 1 — business and causal claim

- State in one sentence how the business earns cash, who pays, and why the relationship persists.
- Name no more than two central assumptions that could be disproved publicly.
- Work only inside two or three industries that can be followed for years; use a different model for banks, property, cyclicals and public REITs.

**Fail:** a thesis needs a policy slogan, a price chart or an undefined “industry boom” to explain earnings.

## Vote 2 — cash, debt and reinvestment

- Review at least three annual reports, the latest two reporting periods and material announcements.
- Reconcile earnings to operating cash flow, receivables, inventory, maintenance/growth capex and working capital.
- Stress net debt, maturity schedule, interest coverage, guarantees, pledges and the plausibility of refinancing.

**Fail:** cash conversion persistently contradicts profit without an evidenced reason; debt/guarantee risk defeats the bear case; or reported growth relies on aggressive acquisitions/receivables.

## Vote 3 — governance and alignment

- Read audit opinion, controlling-holder pledges/reductions, related-party transactions, incentive plan, buyback/dividend record and regulatory inquiries.
- Treat an unexplained non-standard audit, material fund occupation, opaque related party flow or governance deterioration as a veto or a downgrade—not as a discount to be “compensated” by a low multiple.

**Fail:** governance becomes an unpriceable tail risk.

## Vote 4 — valuation and downside

- Build bear/base/bull using revenue, margin, capex, financing and a valuation anchor appropriate to the business; split expected return into earnings/cash-flow delivery, cash distribution and any re-rating rather than hiding them in one PE multiple.
- The base case must remain attractive without heroic multiple expansion; the bear case must fit the investor's separately approved loss budget.

**Fail:** a single historical PE, a broker target or a distant “TAM” is the only valuation support.

## Vote 5 — proof calendar, liquidity and invalidation

- Identify a public 6–18 month proof point: result, cash conversion, order/delivery, utilisation, pricing, deleveraging, distribution or asset-operating KPI.
- Pre-write hard failure (fraud, audit/governance collapse, permanent business/financing impairment) and soft failure (two reporting periods below bear-KPI, an unfulfilled catalyst window, exhausted valuation headroom).
- Test current turnover, expected participation, settlement restrictions and the ability to reduce a position in normal—not ideal—liquidity.

**Fail:** the thesis has no public KPI or assumes a same-day exit at the desired price.

## Evidence-triggered funding, not price-triggered averaging

An illustrative sequence is: (1) initial underwriting passes; (2) next report validates the designated KPI; (3) cash flow/margin confirms the mechanism; (4) re-underwriting shows continuing valuation headroom. Each step requires unchanged or improved evidence. A lower price alone is never an upgrade.
