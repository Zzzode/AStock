# Variant perception and counter-thesis

## Consensus shortcut to reject

“Top private equity earns superior returns, so copying its sectors, buying high-distribution real assets, or adding leverage should create a superior personal portfolio.”

## Why that shortcut fails

1. Private-equity outcome measurement is path-dependent: drawdowns, cash-call timing, appraisal marks, fees, leverage and exit timing differ materially from a liquid public-account return series.
2. The sponsor can negotiate terms, appoint management, alter incentives, control capital expenditure and accept or force illiquidity. A minority investor cannot.
3. China-market settlement, price limits, liquidity and account permissions make an assumed fast exit particularly fragile.
4. The more “institution-like” a retail proposal sounds—private credit, LBO, merger arbitrage, fund secondaries or high yield—the more likely it depends on access, legal structure, diversification or operational resources absent from the personal account.

## Strongest rebuttal to the house view

Even a disciplined public-market analogue may fail to outperform a low-cost diversified benchmark. Public disclosures can lag operating deterioration; a good business can be overvalued; a governance problem can emerge abruptly; and a small private-equity-like direct sleeve can add workload and concentration without an informational edge.

## Decision implication

The framework earns its place only if it improves **loss avoidance, explanation quality and behavioural control**, not because it promises alpha. A user who cannot sustain document review, scenario work and independent challenge should prefer a simpler diversified approach and keep the direct-research sleeve at zero.
