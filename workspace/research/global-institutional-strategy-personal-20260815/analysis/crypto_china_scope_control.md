# China–Crypto Scope Control

The report addresses crypto in two different senses. First, trend, funding, liquidity, custody and counterparty can be studied as economic mechanisms. Second, such study does not create an operational right to buy, sell, lend, stake, borrow, custody or access an offshore venue.

For a China-mainland personal framework, the CSRC-hosted 2026 joint notice (S14) is used to block implementation. The notice states that virtual currency lacks legal-tender status and strictly prohibits virtual-currency-related business in China. This memo is not individual legal advice; it neither decides a reader’s legal position nor constructs a cross-border, technical or wrapper workaround. It also does not claim that every distributed-ledger or approved-infrastructure activity is covered by the same conclusion.

The only current result is `q=0`, zero named crypto assets, zero named crypto products and zero implementation authority. A hypothetical later jurisdiction-specific study would need independent legal/access review, exact vehicle identity, venue/custody/recovery analysis, mark-source controls, liquidity/withdrawal evidence, funding/leverage restrictions, tax records, an approved risk budget and an independent red team.
