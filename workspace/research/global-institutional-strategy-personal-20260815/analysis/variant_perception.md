# Variant Perception and Red-Team Register

| Consensus shortcut | Why it fails | Evidence that would change the conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “A famous manager’s public page is a strategy blueprint.” | Public pages are mandate descriptions, not code, holdings, costs or financing. | Auditable, permitted, complete rule and data evidence—still not a manager-equivalence claim. |
| “Futures eligibility means a CTA is ready.” | Eligibility is not a contract, data, margin, delivery or loss-budget dossier. | Dated broker/exchange/contract evidence plus formal point-in-time paper replay. |
| “A factor screen is a factor premium.” | Look-ahead, survivorship, liquidity, concentration and regime risk can dominate. | Point-in-time data, costs, robust subperiods and failure analysis. |
| “A stop makes the downside bounded.” | Limits, gaps, halts and margin calls can prevent assumed exit. | Stress replay with unavailable fills and explicit escalation plan. |
| “Crypto's 24/7 trading fixes A-share liquidity.” | Jurisdiction, venue, custody, counterparty, funding and withdrawal risk are separate. | A lawful jurisdiction-specific dossier; no mainland workaround. |
| “Private-credit-like yield is safe income.” | Yield can compensate default, liquidity, leverage or counterparty risk. | Contractual rights and recovery/credit evidence, not advertising. |

The red-team conclusion is intentionally asymmetric: evidence may admit a future paper study, but absence of evidence cannot admit a live strategy.
